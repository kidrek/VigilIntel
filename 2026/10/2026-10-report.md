# 🇬🇧 Monthly Synthesis — October 2026

## Threat Level

**🔴🔴🔴🔴🔴 5/5 — Severe**

October 2026 was a severe, multi-vector month: actively exploited edge appliances (Citrix NetScaler, SonicWall SMA1000, Ivanti Connect Secure, PAN-OS, Cisco, Fortinet, Atlassian), identity abuse via AiTM/device-code phishing and ConnectWise service principals, mass data breaches exceeding 200 million records at named victims, ransomware extortion with employee/family pressure, and physical OT impact against French supermarket refrigeration. AI agents and MCP abuse lowered offensive barriers and expanded the attack surface.

## Executive Summary

October 2026 was defined by a severe convergence of mass exploitation of edge and security appliances, industrial-scale data theft and state-sponsored espionage. Adversaries weaponised newly disclosed zero-days within hours—Citrix NetScaler, Cisco, FortiMail, Zimbra, SonicWall SMA1000, Ivanti Connect Secure, PAN-OS, BeyondTrust and Atlassian—many added to CISA KEV, exposing VPN, email and identity gateways as the weakest link. Breach volumes reached hundreds of millions of records, concentrated in healthcare, government and consumer services (Pentagon/DMDC, McKesson, Equifax, Oracle Health, Danish CPR), fuelling identity fraud and downstream extortion. Ransomware remained highly active and data-centric: The Gentlemen, Qilin, Akira, Rhysida, BianLian and Inc Ransom combined edge exploitation, valid accounts and cloud exfiltration, while encryption-free extortion and pressure on employees and families intensified. Law-enforcement wins disrupted some crews but did not reduce overall tempo. State actors sustained espionage and influence: China-nexus groups targeted government, AI-policy and academic entities and exploited legacy CVEs; Russia’s Star Blizzard and UAC-0099 expanded phishing and backdoors against Ukraine and NATO-aligned targets; Iran coupled cyber operations with kinetic strikes and AI-generated influence; North Korea continued IT-worker infiltration and financial theft. Two developments stand out. First, cyber-physical risk became tangible: manipulation of Danfoss refrigeration controllers forced French supermarket systems to 50°C, and Iranian strikes on cloud data centres caused permanent data loss. Second, AI emerged as both attack surface and force multiplier—autonomous agents probed government portals, AI-assisted actors scanned thousands of targets in seconds, and MCP/agent frameworks introduced new identity and supply-chain exposures. Supply-chain compromise via npm, WordPress, GitHub and CI/CD, together with AiTM/device-code phishing and OAuth/service-principal abuse, eroded perimeter and MFA assumptions. The strategic imperative is clear: treat identity as the primary control plane, enforce phishing-resistant FIDO2, remediate KEV edge appliances within 72 hours, segment OT and building systems, govern AI agents and MCP access as privileged identities, harden software supply chains, and prepare for data-theft extortion and employee-directed pressure.

## Regulatory & Legal

October's regulatory signals converged on four themes: operationalisation of cyber-resilience and reporting duties (EU CRA 24-hour vulnerability reporting, Japan Active Cyber Defense, DORA TLPT), hardening of defence supply-chain compliance (CMMC Level 2, DFARS 252.204-7021), acceleration of AI governance (EU AI Act Scientific Panel, EN 18286:2026, US AI Agent Accountability Act, UK parliamentary hearing, DHS DeepSeek ban), and enforcement of data-protection and third-party accountability (CNIL and Garante IQVIA fines, Norway Xandr fine, ICO action against the Metropolitan Police). Regulators are increasingly treating identity, AI agents, third-party platforms and OT exposure as compliance-relevant controls, not just technical issues. Security teams should map KEV remediation, AI/MCP governance and supply-chain evidence to these obligations now.

### PART 2 — Geographic Analysis

### Russia

Russian state and criminal ecosystems combined espionage, influence operations, sanctions evasion and ransomware-adjacent infrastructure. Star Blizzard expanded RedFlick phishing to 100+ organisations, UAC-0099 deployed MatchBoil/MatchWok against Ukraine, and Qilin, The Gentlemen, n0n and RaaS affiliates sustained extortion. Cryptomus sanctions and Meliorator AI bot farms show convergence between information operations and financial laundering.

### China

China-nexus activity spanned state espionage, phishing-as-a-service disruption and criminal fraud. UAT-11587 used Antino against Asian governments, TA419 targeted US AI policy experts with AitM OneDrive phishing, FamousSparrow deployed SparroWocky in Latin America, and Flax Typhoon exploited five legacy CVEs. The FBI seized Flax Typhoon tools, while YAM Store, CoGUI and Iron Man takedowns showed pressure on Chinese phishing infrastructure.

### Iran

Iran coupled cyber operations with kinetic and influence activity. UAE reported cyberattacks paired with every missile or drone strike on ports and energy facilities, while OpenAI disrupted an Iranian network using AI-generated fake journalist personas. APT34/Helix Kitten continued GGMS marketing-prefix phishing and SideTwist variants against Middle East finance, government, energy and telecom.

### North Korea

DPRK activity combined espionage, financial crime and IT-worker infiltration. Kimsuky targeted Ukrainian diplomacy and policy experts with fake conference invitations, while FBI raids in Texas exposed laptop farms and 70+ compromised US companies. The model blends identity fraud, remote access and revenue generation, with overlap into cryptocurrency theft and sanctions evasion.

### United States

The US was both a major target and the centre of defensive, judicial and regulatory action. Pentagon/DMDC, McKesson, Equifax, Raptor Supplies, AT&T and Conduent dominated breach volumes; FBI arrested ShinyHunters-linked suspects, seized Flax Typhoon tools and dismantled DPRK laptop farms. CISA KEV additions, the DHS DeepSeek ban and AI Agent Accountability Act proposal shaped enterprise priorities.

### Other

Kazakhstan-nexus TAG-193/UAT-6275 targeted CIS government entities with diplomatic and environmental intelligence objectives, exploiting WinRAR CVE-2025-8088. UAT-11985 targeted Taiwanese academic researchers with Google AitM phishing. CyberXero used Claude Code to steal 628,000 Ukrainian records, TAG-217 hit 16 South Korean financial institutions, and UNC7072/CLOUDHAZE deployed a Rust RAT against EU oil and gas.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Enforce FIDO2 and Kill Device-Code Flow**
   SMS/TOTP MFA is now routinely bypassed by EvilTokens, UAT-11985 Google AiTM proxying and Scattered Spider session-cookie forgery, while device-code phishing gave ShinyHunters and Scattered Spider persistent access to Microsoft 365 and cloud tenants. Deploy FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware keys (YubiKey 5, Google Titan) for all privileged, developer and CI/CD accounts, disable the OAuth device-code grant in Entra ID and Google Workspace, and revoke all active refresh tokens on rollout. This directly mitigates token theft and AiTM session replay that enabled the FBI FBIJobs.gov and ConnectWise service-principal incidents.

2. **Patch KEV Edge Appliances Within 72 Hours**
   October saw same-day exploitation of Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-88771/88772/88779, SonicWall SMA1000 CVE-2026-102255 (CVSS 10.0), Ivanti Connect Secure CVE-2026-104935, PAN-OS CVE-2026-0322/0257, Cisco CVE-2026-76504/76519, FortiMail CVE-2026-104286, BeyondTrust CVE-2026-102764 and Atlassian CVE-2026-21589. Operate a 72-hour SLA for CISA KEV entries on internet-facing appliances, with compensating controls (WAF virtual patching, management-plane IP allow-listing, MFA on admin portals) when patching slips. These devices are the primary initial-access vector for Warlock, Akira, The Gentlemen and Qilin ransomware operations.

3. **Govern AI Agents and MCP as Privileged Identities**
   ApeX, TornadoC2 and CikaBika abused MCP servers and AI-agent frameworks for post-exploitation, while 'Repeat-After-Me' prompt injection affected LangGraph, AutoGen, ADK 2.0, Strands Agents, CrewAI and Microsoft Agent Framework. Inventory every agent, MCP server and tool credential; enforce least-privilege scoped tokens, human approval for destructive actions, egress allow-lists and per-agent audit logging. Patch AWS Bedrock AgentCore CVE-2026-105812 and Google OSS Agent Mesh CVE-2026-102503, and treat agent output as untrusted input to downstream systems.

4. **Remove OT and BAS from the Internet**
   The Danfoss AK-SM 800/AK-SC 355 campaign that forced refrigeration to 50°C across Intermarché, Coopérative U and E.Leclerc stores demonstrated physical stock destruction from exposed building automation, and Warlock used the K7RKScan.sys BYOVD driver against water and telecom operators. Enforce IEC 62443 zones and conduits, place OT behind brokered remote access with MFA and session recording, and apply NIST SP 1800-45 guidance for water utilities. Assume safety, refrigeration and energy-management controllers are targetable and validate manual override procedures.

5. **Harden the Software Supply Chain End-to-End**
   SlugFest abused npm Trusted Publishing across 1,500+ packages, FakeGit operated 17,610 malicious GitHub repositories, GhostAction stole CI/CD secrets, and MALFEX, Apex Accelerator, Rank Math and 42 RubyGems packages targeted developers. Enforce SLSA provenance and signed commits, pin GitHub Actions by commit SHA, require OIDC-based short-lived publishing tokens, and continuously scan with gitleaks, TruffleHog and Socket.dev. Patch GitLab CVE-2026-97229 (CVSS 10.0) and Docker Hub CVE-2026-105445, which directly undermine CI/CD integrity.

6. **Prepare for Data-Theft Extortion and Staff Safety**
   Silent Ransom Group extorted ~$207M from 27 law firms without encryption, The Gentlemen and BianLian pressured employees and families, and Zscaler reports data theft up 275.8% while ransom payments fell 15.8%. Build a data-theft playbook covering legal notification, dark-web monitoring, executive and employee protection, and pre-agreed negotiation and law-enforcement contacts. Test offline immutable backups (3-2-1-1-0) and identity-plane recovery, because encryption is no longer the primary leverage.

7. **Govern MSP, SaaS and Cloud Identity Risk**
   The ConnectWise service-principal incident exposed Microsoft 365 tenants, the FBI breach flowed through an Accenture-managed Oracle PeopleSoft instance, and The Gentlemen reached South African insurers via MIP Holdings. Enforce contractual security baselines for MSPs, require just-in-time delegated administration, audit third-party OAuth grants and service principals quarterly, and monitor for anomalous Graph and Exchange Online activity. Third-party aggregation points now yield more records than direct compromises.

8. **Build AI-Assisted Detection and Crypto-Agility**
   Anthropic Mythos (129,000+ verified vulnerabilities) and Google PageBreak (500 web flaws) are compressing patch windows, while Forescout finds only 6% of IoMT and 16% of OT assets PQC-ready and Let's Encrypt moves to 64-day certificates. Invest in AI-assisted triage with human validation, reachability-based prioritisation, and automated certificate lifecycle management (ACME, cert-manager, Venafi). Start a post-quantum migration roadmap for long-lived OT, identity and signing infrastructure now.

## Operational Recommendations

1. **Hunt ConnectWise Service-Principal Abuse**
   Query Microsoft Graph and Entra ID audit logs for new service principals, credential additions and OAuth grants created after 1 October 2026, focusing on ConnectWise, Exchange Online, SharePoint and Teams scopes. Use `AuditLogs | where OperationName in ('Add service principal','Add service principal credentials','Consent to application')` and correlate with sign-ins from unfamiliar ASNs. Revoke suspicious credentials, remove excessive Graph permissions and force re-consent for affected tenants.

2. **Patch Listed Edge Appliances Immediately**
   Apply vendor fixes for Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-88771/88772/88779, SonicWall SMA1000 CVE-2026-102255/102256, Ivanti Connect Secure CVE-2026-104935 (22.8R2.5, 25.1.1.2, 25.1.3.1), PAN-OS CVE-2026-0322/0257, Cisco CVE-2026-76504/76519, FortiMail CVE-2026-104286, BeyondTrust CVE-2026-102764 and Atlassian CVE-2026-21589. After patching, hunt for web shells in `/netscaler/ns_gui`, `/var/tmp`, IIS `wwwroot` and Atlassian `webapps`, and rotate all local appliance credentials. Treat any unpatched internet-facing instance as compromised.

3. **Disable Device-Code Flow and Revoke Sessions**
   In Entra ID set `Authentication Methods > Policies` to block device-code authentication for all users except break-glass accounts, and in Google Workspace disable the OAuth device flow under `Security > API controls`. Revoke all refresh tokens and sign out users tenant-wide, then require FIDO2 re-registration. Monitor sign-in logs for `authenticationProtocol: deviceCode` and for AiTM indicators such as impossible travel and token replay from new ASNs.

4. **Audit npm, GitHub and CI/CD Dependencies**
   Run `npm audit --production`, `npm ls --all` and Socket.dev or Phylum scans across all repositories, and search for the malicious packages tlxbnhd, tldriver, mxdriver, img-to-native and SlugFest-published names. Rotate all npm tokens, disable legacy publish tokens in favour of OIDC Trusted Publishing, and pin GitHub Actions to commit SHAs. Scan history with `gitleaks detect --source . --log-opts="--all"` and TruffleHog, then rotate any secret found in the 543,699 credentials exposed across GitHub repositories this month.

5. **Remove OT and BAS Internet Exposure**
   Query Shodan and Censys for Danfoss AK-SM 800/AK-SC 355, LG webOS, Johnson Controls EasyIO FS32 (CVE-2026-71449) and building-management protocols exposed on ports 80/443/47808/502, then block at the perimeter. Enforce allow-listed brokered access with MFA and session recording, and apply Danfoss firmware updates plus network segmentation for refrigeration controllers. Validate that manual override and safe-temperature procedures work without remote connectivity.

6. **Detect ClickFix, Romulus and CikaBika Chains**
   Monitor DNS for TXT queries to newly registered domains and for r2.dev, vercel.app, pages.dev and requestrepo.com subdomains, and alert on PowerShell `-enc` and `mshta` execution from browser-spawned processes. Hunt for Run-key persistence, scheduled tasks named to mimic Windows updates, and ScreenConnect/Action1 MSI installations with expired certificates. Block clipboard-injection lures and user training on fake CAPTCHA and 'verify you are human' prompts.

7. **Hunt AiTM and Session-Cookie Theft**
   Correlate Entra ID and Okta sign-in logs for successful authentications from new ASNs immediately after a phishing click, especially where MFA was satisfied but the user agent changed. Search for `session cookie` replay, anomalous `ESTSAUTH`/`ESTSAUTHPERSISTENT` issuance and OAuth token grants to unfamiliar applications. Revoke sessions, reset credentials and require FIDO2 for any account showing token replay or device-code sign-in.

8. **Validate Offline and Identity Backups**
   Test restoration of at least one domain controller, Entra Connect server and critical file share from immutable offline media, verifying that backups are not domain-joined and cannot be deleted by production credentials. Confirm 3-2-1-1-0 coverage for SaaS data (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace) and for OT configuration backups. Given Qilin, The Gentlemen and BianLian's recovery inhibition (T1490), an untested backup is equivalent to no backup.

9. **Monitor Leak Sites and Employee Threats**
   Track ShinyHunters, Qilin, The Gentlemen, BianLian, Rhysida, Inc Ransom, Play, Orova, Umbra and DragonForce leak sites for your brand, subsidiaries and suppliers, and set alerts for employee names and executive contacts. Brief HR and legal on extortion calls to employees and families, and establish a single point of contact for law enforcement. Pre-draft notification templates for GDPR 72-hour and CRA 24-hour reporting obligations.

10. **Patch Windows Shell and Exchange Out-of-Band**
   Deploy the October fixes for Windows Shell CVE-2026-96951 (Windows 10/11, Server 2012 R2-2025, Outlook.com) and Exchange Server CVE-2026-96940 (SE, 2019, 2016 on-premises), which enable command injection and privilege escalation. Verify patch level with `Get-HotFix` and `Get-ExchangeServer | fl Name,AdminDisplayVersion`, and restrict Outlook.com and OWA exposure until confirmed. Hunt for suspicious `rundll32`, `mshta` and Exchange PowerShell activity post-patch.

11. **Deploy SigmaHQ Rules for DNS Exfiltration**
   Import the latest SigmaHQ rules covering DNS TXT staging, renamed `schtasks`, WSL abuse, Kerberos ticket anomalies and Azure subscription permission changes into your SIEM. Tune for false positives using 30 days of baseline data, then alert on requestrepo.com, unusual TXT record volume and encoded PowerShell from Office processes. These detections close gaps left by signature-only controls against ClickFix and CrocoRat.

12. **Rotate Credentials Exposed in Public Repositories**
   Run GitHub secret scanning and GitGuardian across all organisations, then rotate every AWS, Azure, GCP, npm, PyPI and database credential found, prioritising those with CI/CD or production access. Enforce push protection and pre-commit hooks (gitleaks, detect-secrets) and require short-lived OIDC credentials. The 543,699 credentials exposed this month and the GhostAction CI/CD theft campaign make rotation and prevention urgent.

### PARTIE 2 — Analyse Géographique

### Russie

Les écosystèmes étatiques et criminels russes ont combiné espionnage, opérations d'influence, contournement des sanctions et infrastructures proches du rançongiciel. Star Blizzard a étendu le phishing RedFlick à plus de 100 organisations, UAC-0099 a déployé MatchBoil/MatchWok contre l'Ukraine, et Qilin, The Gentlemen, n0n et affiliés RaaS ont maintenu l'extorsion. Les sanctions Cryptomus et les fermes de bots IA Meliorator montrent une convergence entre opérations d'influence et blanchiment financier.

### Chine

L'activité liée à la Chine a couvert espionnage étatique, perturbation de phishing-as-a-service et fraude criminelle. UAT-11587 a utilisé Antino contre des gouvernements asiatiques, TA419 a ciblé des experts américains en politiques IA via phishing AitM OneDrive, FamousSparrow a déployé SparroWocky en Amérique latine, et Flax Typhoon a exploité cinq CVE héritées. Le FBI a saisi les outils de Flax Typhoon, tandis que les démantèlements YAM Store, CoGUI et Iron Man montrent la pression sur les infrastructures de phishing chinoises.

### Iran

L'Iran a couplé opérations cyber, activité cinétique et influence. Les Émirats ont rapporté des cyberattaques associées à chaque frappe de missile ou drone sur ports et installations énergétiques, tandis qu'OpenAI a démantelé un réseau iranien utilisant de faux journalistes générés par IA. APT34/Helix Kitten a poursuivi le phishing au prétexte marketing GGMS et les variantes SideTwist contre finance, gouvernement, énergie et télécoms au Moyen-Orient.

### Corée du Nord

L'activité nord-coréenne a combiné espionnage, criminalité financière et infiltration de travailleurs IT. Kimsuky a ciblé des experts ukrainiens en diplomatie et politique via de fausses invitations à des conférences, tandis que les perquisitions du FBI au Texas ont exposé des fermes d'ordinateurs et plus de 70 entreprises américaines compromises. Le modèle mêle fraude d'identité, accès distant et génération de revenus, avec recoupement avec le vol de cryptomonnaies et le contournement des sanctions.

### États-Unis

Les États-Unis ont été à la fois une cible majeure et le centre de l'action défensive, judiciaire et réglementaire. Pentagone/DMDC, McKesson, Equifax, Raptor Supplies, AT&T et Conduent ont dominé les volumes de fuites ; le FBI a arrêté des suspects liés à ShinyHunters, saisi les outils de Flax Typhoon et démantelé des fermes d'ordinateurs nord-coréennes. Les ajouts au KEV, l'interdiction de DeepSeek par le DHS et la proposition d'AI Agent Accountability Act ont façonné les priorités des entreprises.

### Autre

TAG-193/UAT-6275, lié au Kazakhstan, a ciblé des entités gouvernementales de la CEI avec des objectifs de renseignement diplomatique et environnemental, en exploitant WinRAR CVE-2025-8088. UAT-11985 a ciblé des chercheurs académiques taïwanais via phishing Google AitM. CyberXero a utilisé Claude Code pour voler 628 000 dossiers ukrainiens, TAG-217 a frappé 16 institutions financières sud-coréennes, et UNC7072/CLOUDHAZE a déployé un RAT Rust contre le secteur oil and gas de l'UE.

**Techniques en déclin :**
- Détection de fichiers par signature et réputation seules, contournée par ClickFix, l'abus de RMM, le module stomping et la redirection de thread (Exoproc).
- SMS et TOTP comme MFA principale, vaincus par EvilTokens, la proxification AiTM d'UAT-11985 et le phishing par code d'appareil.
- Authentification par mot de passe seul et confiance implicite au LAN, invalidées par l'abus de service principals, le vol de jetons OAuth et la dispersion des identités cloud.
- Défense périmétrique seule, contournée par la compromission npm/GitHub/MCP et l'héritage d'agents tiers.
- Priorisation des correctifs sur le seul CVSS, supplantée par CISA KEV, EPSS et l'analyse de reachability face à 58-69 vulnérabilités par jour.
- Hypothèses d'air gap pour l'OT et l'automatisation du bâtiment, contredites par les contrôleurs Danfoss et LG webOS exposés sur Internet.
- Cycles de correctifs manuels et lents pour les équipements de bordure, intenables face à l'exploitation le jour même de Citrix, SonicWall, Ivanti et PAN-OS.
- Livraison par documents Office avec macros, remplacée par ClickFix, PDF /OpenAction et chaînes LNK/ISO.
- Points de C2 statiques codés en dur, remplacés par des contrats intelligents Web3 (EtherHiding), des résolveurs dead-drop GitHub et le staging DNS TXT.
- Injection de DLL classique (CreateRemoteThread + LoadLibrary), remplacée par la redirection de thread et l'instrumentation hors processus.
- Extorsion par rançongiciel avec chiffrement seul, déplacée par l'extorsion par vol de données et la pression psychologique (Silent Ransom Group, The Gentlemen, BianLian).
- E-mails de phishing simples et typosquats évidents, remplacés par des leurres contextuels générés par IA et un hébergement cloud légitime (r2.dev, vercel.app, Google Sites, Replit).
- Confiance dans le passage SPF/DKIM/DMARC comme preuve de légitimité, déjouée par le phishing via services de confiance et les domaines homoglyphes.
- Full Disk Access accordé globalement sur macOS et systèmes hérités non corrigés (Trimble Cityworks 91 % non corrigé, fuite FBI via Oracle PeopleSoft).

## Recommandations Stratégiques

1. **Imposer FIDO2 et supprimer le flux device-code**
   La MFA SMS/TOTP est désormais régulièrement contournée par EvilTokens, la proxification Google AiTM d'UAT-11985 et la falsification de cookies de session de Scattered Spider, tandis que le phishing par code d'appareil a donné à ShinyHunters et Scattered Spider un accès persistant aux tenants Microsoft 365 et au cloud. Déployez des clés matérielles FIDO2/WebAuthn (YubiKey 5, Google Titan) pour tous les comptes privilégiés, développeurs et CI/CD, désactivez l'octroi OAuth device-code dans Entra ID et Google Workspace, et révoquez tous les jetons de rafraîchissement actifs au déploiement. Cela atténue directement le vol de jetons et le rejeu de session AiTM à l'origine des incidents FBI FBIJobs.gov et ConnectWise.

2. **Corriger les équipements KEV sous 72 heures**
   Octobre a connu une exploitation le jour même de Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-88771/88772/88779, SonicWall SMA1000 CVE-2026-102255 (CVSS 10.0), Ivanti Connect Secure CVE-2026-104935, PAN-OS CVE-2026-0322/0257, Cisco CVE-2026-76504/76519, FortiMail CVE-2026-104286, BeyondTrust CVE-2026-102764 et Atlassian CVE-2026-21589. Appliquez un SLA de 72 heures pour les entrées CISA KEV sur les équipements exposés à Internet, avec des contrôles compensatoires (virtual patching WAF, allow-list IP du plan de gestion, MFA sur les portails d'administration) en cas de retard. Ces équipements sont le principal vecteur d'accès initial des opérations Warlock, Akira, The Gentlemen et Qilin.

3. **Gouverner les agents IA et MCP comme des identités privilégiées**
   ApeX, TornadoC2 et CikaBika ont abusé des serveurs MCP et des frameworks d'agents IA pour la post-exploitation, tandis que l'injection de prompt « Repeat-After-Me » affectait LangGraph, AutoGen, ADK 2.0, Strands Agents, CrewAI et Microsoft Agent Framework. Inventoriez chaque agent, serveur MCP et identifiant d'outil ; imposez des jetons à privilège minimal, une approbation humaine pour les actions destructrices, des allow-lists d'egress et une journalisation d'audit par agent. Corrigez AWS Bedrock AgentCore CVE-2026-105812 et Google OSS Agent Mesh CVE-2026-102503, et traitez la sortie d'agent comme une entrée non fiable pour les systèmes en aval.

4. **Retirer l'OT et le BAS d'Internet**
   La campagne Danfoss AK-SM 800/AK-SC 355 qui a poussé la réfrigération à 50 °C dans les magasins Intermarché, Coopérative U et E.Leclerc a démontré la destruction physique de stocks à partir d'une automatisation du bâtiment exposée, et Warlock a utilisé le pilote BYOVD K7RKScan.sys contre l'eau et les télécoms. Appliquez les zones et conduits IEC 62443, placez l'OT derrière un accès distant brokerisé avec MFA et enregistrement de session, et appliquez le guide NIST SP 1800-45 pour l'eau. Considérez les contrôleurs de sécurité, de froid et de gestion énergétique comme ciblables et validez les procédures de reprise manuelle.

5. **Durcir la chaîne d'approvisionnement logicielle de bout en bout**
   SlugFest a abusé de npm Trusted Publishing sur plus de 1 500 paquets, FakeGit opérait 17 610 dépôts GitHub malveillants, GhostAction volait des secrets CI/CD, et MALFEX, Apex Accelerator, Rank Math et 42 paquets RubyGems ciblaient les développeurs. Imposez la provenance SLSA et les commits signés, épinglez les GitHub Actions par SHA de commit, exigez des jetons de publication éphémères via OIDC et scannez en continu avec gitleaks, TruffleHog et Socket.dev. Corrigez GitLab CVE-2026-97229 (CVSS 10.0) et Docker Hub CVE-2026-105445, qui compromettent directement l'intégrité de la CI/CD.

6. **Préparer l'extorsion par vol de données et la sécurité des employés**
   Le Silent Ransom Group a extorqué ~207 M$ à 27 cabinets d'avocats sans chiffrement, The Gentlemen et BianLian ont fait pression sur les employés et leurs familles, et Zscaler rapporte +275,8 % de vol de données contre -15,8 % de paiements de rançon. Construisez un playbook de vol de données couvrant la notification légale, la surveillance du dark web, la protection des dirigeants et des employés, et des contacts pré-agréés de négociation et d'enquête. Testez les sauvegardes immuables hors ligne (3-2-1-1-0) et la restauration du plan identité, car le chiffrement n'est plus le principal levier.

7. **Gouverner le risque MSP, SaaS et identité cloud**
   L'incident de service principals ConnectWise a exposé des tenants Microsoft 365, la violation du FBI est passée par une instance Oracle PeopleSoft gérée par Accenture, et The Gentlemen a atteint des assureurs sud-africains via MIP Holdings.
